A missing tooth changes more than your smile

When you lose a tooth, your oral health is usually better off if you replace it. Missing teeth change your bite. They make speaking and chewing harder, they shift extra load onto the teeth you have left, and that strain can surface as jaw muscle pain and headaches. Losing a tooth changes your appearance too, and everybody feels that one. When every lower tooth is gone, a denture that just rests on the gums has nothing to hold onto. Anchoring it on dental implants gives it a grip your gums never could.

Ball attachment: two implants, and the honest trade-offs

The simplest option puts two implants in your lower jaw and fits your denture to snap onto them. Chewing gets far more stable than with a denture alone. Dr. Burns will also tell you what the brochures skip: some movement remains, and if food particles get caught underneath, especially seeds, you'll feel sore spots. Like all removable replacement teeth, a ball attachment denture still needs periodic adjustment appointments. For many patients, that trade is still worth it.

The overdenture: four to six implants and a custom support bar

The second option is what dentists call an overdenture. Depending on your jaw's size and shape, Dr. Burns places four to six implants. After healing finishes, he connects them with a custom-made support bar. Your denture is built with special internal retention clips that snap firmly onto that bar, so it barely moves at all. It still comes out when you want it to, which keeps cleaning and maintenance easy. Most loose-denture patients land here.

Screw retained: a permanent denture that doesn't touch your gums

The third option places five or more implants and attaches a permanent denture, secured by screws or clasps to the support posts or bar. It doesn't rest on your gum tissue at all, so you clean under it without removing it. It only leaves your mouth at maintenance visits, in the dental chair. Fair warning: cleaning under a fixed denture takes more time and more dexterity than soaking a removable one. Patients who want teeth that stay in choose it anyway, and rarely look back.

Missing all of your upper teeth? You may not need the palate

The same range of options works for the upper jaw, with one difference: upper bone is softer than lower, so more implants are usually needed to support your new teeth. Here's the payoff. With enough implants, Dr. Burns can eliminate the need to cover the roof of your mouth the way a complete denture does. You taste your food fully again and feel its temperature, and the whole thing feels more natural. The denture stays removable, which makes cleaning it and the support bar much easier.

What the implants underneath give you

Whichever attachment you choose, the implants themselves do quiet work every day:

  • Improved confidence, because the denture can't surprise you
  • Stability during eating, even foods you'd given up
  • Bone and gum preservation where teeth are missing
  • Improved dental hygiene, easier cleaning routines
  • Superior esthetics, teeth that sit where they should
  • Nutritional benefits, because you chew what's good for you

Frequently Asked Questions

Ball, bar, or screws: your questions, answered

How many implants does an implant retained denture need?

It depends on the option. A ball attachment denture snaps onto two implants in the lower jaw. A bar attachment overdenture uses four to six, depending on your jaw's size and shape. A screw retained denture needs five or more. Upper jaws usually need more implants than lower jaws because the bone up top is softer. A 3D scan at your free consultation gives you your number.

Will a two-implant snap-on denture still move?

Some, yes. A ball attachment denture chews far more steadily than a denture alone, but there's still movement, and sore spots can develop if food particles, especially seeds, get caught under it. Like all removable replacement teeth, it needs periodic adjustment appointments. Dr. Burns explains these trade-offs before you choose, not after.

Is there an option that never comes out?

Yes, the screw retained denture. Five or more implants hold a permanent denture that's secured by screws or clasps to the support posts or bar. It doesn't touch the gum tissue, so you clean under it without removing it, and it only comes out at maintenance visits. Cleaning it in place takes more time and dexterity, but patients who want a permanent denture prefer it.

What is an overdenture?

An overdenture is the bar attachment option. Four to six implants heal in your jaw, then get connected with a custom-made support bar. Your denture carries special internal retention clips that snap firmly onto that bar. It's much more stable than a two-implant snap-on, allows very little movement, and still comes out for easy cleaning and maintenance.

Can an upper implant denture skip the roof of my mouth?

Often, yes. Upper jawbone is softer, so more implants are needed, but with enough of them the denture no longer has to cover your palate like a complete denture does. You get your full sense of taste back, feel the temperature of your food, and the denture feels more natural. It stays removable, which makes cleaning the support bar and the denture much easier.

Why replace missing lower teeth at all?

Missing teeth affect your bite and your ability to speak and chew. They add load to your remaining teeth and can cause jaw muscle pain and headaches, and of course they change your appearance. Replacing them protects your oral health, and anchoring the replacement on implants also preserves the bone and gums underneath.
