Do I have enough bone for dental implants?

It comes down to your socket walls. After tooth extraction, if the walls of the socket are thick, they'll usually fill naturally with bone in two to three months. When the walls are thin, like the ones around your upper and lower front teeth, that healing isn't as predictable. So in those cases a bone graft is often placed at the time of extraction, to help your body fill the empty socket with bone. That one step maintains the width and volume of bone you'll need for implant placement several months later.

Lost the tooth years ago? The ridge can be rebuilt

If your tooth was removed many years ago, the bony ridge underneath may now be extremely thin, and there may be inadequate bone for an implant. In that case, graft material is placed next to the thin bone and allowed to heal for up to six months. Once the graft has fused to your pre-existing bone, the ridge is re-entered and the implant placed. It sounds bigger than it feels. Bone grafting is usually a relatively comfortable office procedure, and many different bone-grafting materials are available, including your own bone.

The “sinus grafting procedure,” explained

Sometimes the sinus cavities in your upper jaw are very large, or very low, and extend into the tooth-bearing areas. That's common when back upper teeth were removed many years before, and it limits the bone available for an implant. The fix is a “sinus grafting procedure.” The membrane that lines the sinus is located and gently elevated, then bone is added to restore the bone height, so dental implants of an adequate length can be placed. Most often it's performed in the office with local anesthesia, perhaps with sedation, and it often happens at the time of implant placement.

What actually goes into a graft

Many different bone-grafting materials are available, including your own bone. Which one fits your case depends on where the graft is going and how much rebuilding your jaw needs. That's a conversation, not a guess. Dr. Burns pulls up your 3D scan, points at the exact spot, and walks you through the choice before anything gets scheduled.

An office procedure, not a hospital stay

People hear “bone graft” and picture an operating room. The reality is calmer. Grafting is usually a relatively comfortable office procedure, done with local anesthesia in the same New Market office where Valley families get their checkups. Want sedation? You can have it. Most patients are surprised by how ordinary the visit feels.

The timeline, start to smile

A healthy socket fills with bone in two to three months. A staged ridge graft heals for up to six before the ridge is re-entered and the implant placed. And a sinus graft often happens the same day as implant placement, which folds two procedures into one visit. Frequently Asked Questions

What patients ask before a graft

Does bone grafting hurt?

Bone grafting is usually a relatively comfortable office procedure. It's done with local anesthesia, and sedation is available if you want it. Most patients tell us the anticipation was worse than the appointment.

How long does a bone graft heal before the implant goes in?

It depends on the graft. One placed against an extremely thin ridge heals for up to six months. After it has fused to your pre-existing bone, the ridge is re-entered and the implant placed. A graft placed in a fresh socket typically supports implant placement several months later.

Do I have enough bone for dental implants?

Only a 3D scan can say for sure. If the walls of your socket are thick after an extraction, they'll usually fill naturally with bone in two to three months. Thin walls, like the ones around your upper and lower front teeth, heal less predictably. That's when a graft placed at the time of extraction preserves the width and volume of bone your implant will need.

What is a sinus grafting procedure?

When the sinus cavities in your upper jaw are very large or very low and extend into the tooth-bearing areas, there may not be enough bone height for an implant. In a sinus grafting procedure, the membrane lining the sinus is located and elevated, and bone is added to restore the height so implants of an adequate length can be placed. It's often done at the same time as implant placement, in the office, with local anesthesia and perhaps sedation.

What material is used in a bone graft?

Many different bone-grafting materials are available, including your own bone. Dr. Burns reviews your 3D scan with you and recommends the material that fits your case at your free consultation.
