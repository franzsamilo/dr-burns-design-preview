Missing All of Your Lower Teeth

Although many patients have no problem wearing an upper denture, some find it difficult to wear and eat with a lower denture. The lower jaw offers a plate little to hold onto, and every bite tries to shove it around.

If you're missing all of your lower teeth, several implant-supported replacement options can hold your new teeth steady. They run from a simple two-implant snap-on denture to a full set of individually replaced teeth, and Dr. Burns walks you through each one below.

Ball Attachment Denture: Two Implants, One Snap

The entry point is a Ball Attachment Denture. Dr. Burns sets two implants into the lower jaw, and the denture clicks down onto them. Chewing becomes far less of a balancing act than it ever was with a plate alone.

He'll level with you about what this option won't do, too. A little movement is part of the deal. Sore spots can flare when food particles, especially seeds, work their way beneath the plate. And as with any removable replacement teeth, count on periodic adjustment appointments.

Bar Attachment Denture: The Overdenture

Next up the ladder sits the Bar Attachment Denture, what dentists call an overdenture. Four to six implants go in, the exact count set by the size and shape of your jaw. Once the bone has healed, a custom-made support bar ties them together, and special internal retention clips inside the denture bite onto that bar and hang on.

The upgrade buys you a denture that hardly shifts. It lifts out any time you choose, so cleaning and maintenance never turn into a chore.

Two implants or ten? Your bone casts the deciding vote at a free 3D scan. Take the Quiz

Screw Retained Denture: Teeth That Never Come Out

Choose the Screw Retained Denture and your teeth stop being something you park in a glass at night. Five or more implants anchor a permanent denture, bolted down by screws or clasps to the support posts or bar. Because it doesn't touch the gum tissue, you brush beneath it while it stays put. Dr. Burns removes it only at maintenance visits.

Know this going in: getting a brush beneath a set-in-place denture asks extra time and dexterity of you. For lots of folks, waking with teeth already in answers that question for good.

Individual Implants: The Closest Match to Natural Teeth

The final option rebuilds every tooth on its own, so your new teeth appear to grow straight out of your gum tissue. Expect eight or more implants. Separate abutments, or support posts, are made for each one, and individual crowns go on tooth by tooth. The teeth are often joined together for sinus grafting, which restores bone height, strength, and support.

It's the most costly option, because it calls for the most implants and the most individual tooth fabrication, and the current size and shape of your jawbone can limit what's possible. That's exactly what the 3D scan is for.

What If You're Missing All of Your Upper Teeth?

A similar range of treatment options exists for the upper jaw, with one wrinkle: upper bone is softer than the bone below, so carrying the same set of teeth usually calls for extra implants.

Get the count right, though, and something wonderful becomes possible. The plate that normally blankets the roof of your mouth can disappear from the design altogether. Food tastes like food again. Your sense of temperature returns along with your sense of taste, and the whole denture feels closer to natural teeth. It remains removable, which keeps the support bar and denture simple to clean. If your goal is upper teeth that never come out, individual upper implants usually run eight to ten, with abutments and individual crowns placed once healing finishes.

Frequently Asked Questions

The questions people ask when every tooth is gone

How many implants does it take to replace all of my lower teeth?

Each choice carries its own count. Ball Attachment Denture: two. Bar Attachment Denture, the overdenture: four to six, set by the size and shape of your jaw. Screw Retained Denture: five or more. Rebuilding every tooth individually: eight or more. A 3D scan turns those ranges into your specific number.

Will a two-implant snap-on denture stop the movement completely?

No, and Dr. Burns says so before you choose it. Chewing gets far steadier than with a plate alone, but a little movement is part of the deal, and trapped food particles, especially seeds, can raise sore spots. Count on periodic adjustment appointments, the same as any removable replacement teeth.

Why does the upper jaw usually need more implants than the lower?

Upper bone is softer, so each implant carries less load and the arch asks for extra support. Rebuilding the upper arch tooth by tooth usually calls for eight to ten implants, with abutments and individual crowns following once the bone heals.

Can I get an upper denture that doesn't cover the roof of my mouth?

Often, yes. When enough implants can be placed, the acrylic that would normally blanket your palate gets left out of the design. Your full sense of taste and temperature comes back, and the denture feels far closer to natural teeth. It remains removable, so the support bar and denture rinse clean in seconds.

What's the difference between a bar attachment denture and a screw retained denture?

A Bar Attachment Denture rides on a custom-made support bar, held by internal retention clips, and you pop it off yourself for cleaning. A Screw Retained Denture is the permanent version. Screws or clasps lock it to the posts or bar, it doesn't touch the gum tissue, and you brush beneath it in place. Only Dr. Burns takes it off, at maintenance visits.
