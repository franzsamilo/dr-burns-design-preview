What are dental implants?

A natural tooth has two basic parts: a crown, the visible part you chew with, and a root that holds the tooth securely under the gum, anchored into the jaw. Implant-supported replacement teeth have the same parts. The difference is that the implant root is made of titanium, the same time-tested material surgeons use for artificial joints. When you lose a tooth, you lose both root and crown, so before the crown can come back, the root is replaced with a small dental implant.

Bone, abutment, crown: how the pieces stack

After placement, time is allowed for bone to heal and grow around the dental implant. The bone bonds with the titanium, creating a strong foundation for artificial teeth. A support post, called an abutment, is then placed on the implant, and your new replacement tooth, a crown, goes on top of the abutment. And in many cases you won't wait with a gap: a temporary replacement tooth can be attached to the implant immediately after it's placed.

Surgical advances that cut down your procedures

Recent advances in dental implant technology have opened the door for single stage implants. These don't require a second procedure to uncover them, though they do need a minimum of six weeks of healing time before artificial teeth are placed. There are even situations where the implant can be placed at the same time as the tooth extraction, further minimizing your number of surgical procedures.

Fitted and measured to feel like your own

We're experts in making sure that prosthetic teeth, dental implants included, look and feel just like your natural ones. By carefully fitting and measuring with the help of the newest imaging technology, Dr. Burns is able to ensure optimal function and aesthetics. Your bite, your speech, and the shade of the new crown all get checked before anything is called finished.

Missing every tooth in an arch? Still fixable

If all of your teeth are missing, a variety of treatment options are available to support the replacement teeth, from a full arch fixed on a handful of implants to teeth placed the same day as surgery. The starting point is the same either way: a scan, an honest conversation, and a plan built around your jaw, not a catalog.

Frequently Asked Questions

Roots, abutments, and healing time, explained

How long do single stage implants take to heal?

Single stage implants don't require a second procedure to uncover them, but they do require a minimum of six weeks of healing time before artificial teeth are placed. Dr. Burns confirms your implant has fully bonded with the bone before your final teeth go on.

Can an implant be placed the same day as a tooth extraction?

In some situations, yes. Placing the implant at the same time as the tooth extraction further minimizes your number of surgical procedures. A 3D scan at your consultation tells Dr. Burns whether your case qualifies for same-day placement.

Will I have a gap in my smile while the implant heals?

Usually not. In many cases a temporary replacement tooth can be attached to the dental implant immediately after it's placed, so you leave the office with a tooth in the space while the bone bonds underneath.

What are dental implants?

A dental implant is a small titanium replacement for the root of a missing tooth. Titanium is the same time-tested material surgeons use for artificial joints. Once your bone bonds with the implant, a support post and a new crown go on top, so you get back both parts of the tooth you lost, root and crown.

What is an abutment?

An abutment is the small support post that's placed on the implant after your bone has healed and grown around it. Your replacement crown attaches to the abutment, not directly to the implant itself. Think of it as the connector between the titanium root and the visible tooth.
