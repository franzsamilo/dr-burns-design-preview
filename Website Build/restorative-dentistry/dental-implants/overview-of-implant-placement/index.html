Overview of Dental Implant Placement

Everything on this page comes from what actually happens in Dr. Burns' operatory: the surgery, the healing, the timing, and the count.

The surgical procedure

The procedure to place a dental implant takes 30 to 60 minutes for one implant and only 2 to 3 hours for multiple implants. The number of appointments and the time required vary from patient to patient, and Dr. Burns brings great precision and attention to the details of your case.

Prior to surgery, you may receive antibiotics, and for greater comfort, intravenous sedation or general anesthesia. These options are discussed with you at your consultation. A local anesthetic numbs the area, then a small incision in the gum tissue reveals the bone, special instruments create space, and the titanium implant is gently inserted. The top of the implant is often visible through the gum, though sometimes it's better in the early stages of healing to keep it covered by the gum tissue.

Six stages, from tooth loss to a restored tooth

Healing after implant placement

Now the healing begins. How long it takes varies from person to person, depending on the quality and quantity of your bone, and in some cases implants may be restored immediately after they're placed. After the initial phase of healing, Dr. Burns places an abutment (support post) or a healing cap onto the implant during a brief follow-up visit. That lets the gum tissue mature and provides access to the implant.

Occasionally, impressions are made the day the implant is placed, so your crown is ready the moment you've healed. Expect one to four follow-up appointments to make sure your mouth is healing well, and whether it's one tooth or all of your teeth being replaced, Dr. Burns completes the restoration by fitting the replacement crown to the implant himself.

The soft tissue graft: a small step for natural-looking gums

Sometimes it helps to perform a soft tissue graft around the implant. A small amount of gum tissue is moved from one part of your mouth to the area around the implant, and the payoff is stronger, more easily cleaned and natural appearing gum tissue. It sounds like a bigger deal than it is. Most often it's a brief and relatively comfortable procedure, and it's the kind of finishing detail that makes an implant blend in instead of standing out.

When are dental implants placed?

Dental implants are often placed several months after the extraction of a tooth. At times, an implant may be placed immediately after an extraction. That involves a little more risk, but it simplifies the process, since you won't have to wait for another appointment to place the implant. When infection or other problems with the bone are present, immediate placement isn't the best treatment.

If your tooth has been missing for some time, the adjacent support bone is likely to grow thinner and shrink, because the root of a natural tooth has to be present to stimulate the bone. As much as one third of your jaw's thickness can be lost in the year following tooth extraction. If you're missing enough bone, you may benefit from having additional bone grafted into the area, so the implant is adequately supported when it's placed in the jaw.

How many implants do I need?

Most frequently, one implant is placed per missing tooth. Many of the larger teeth in the back of your jaws have two or three roots, though, so the most common approach there is to replace missing back teeth with larger implants that can carry the heavier chewing load. Your 3D scan gives Dr. Burns the exact count and position for your case, and he walks you through it before anything is scheduled.
