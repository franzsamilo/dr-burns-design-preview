Teeth to smile with while your implants heal

Many options are available, and they're tailored to your specific requirements. If you need a replacement tooth during healing, we can make temporary removable teeth or a temporary bridge. If all of your teeth are missing, we can usually modify your present complete denture or make you a new temporary denture.

Prefer teeth that don't come out? Temporary transitional implants can usually be placed along with the permanent ones, with temporary teeth made and inserted the same day. Depending on your situation, some implants can even be "loaded" immediately, meaning a temporary or permanent replacement tooth goes on the day of placement, or shortly after.

The potential problems, without the sugarcoating

It's natural to worry about pain, but most patients don't experience severe or significant pain after these procedures. You'll go home with prescribed pain medication and antibiotics to make recovery as easy as possible. Occasionally, a post-operative infection develops and needs additional antibiotic treatment, and in rare cases an adjacent tooth can be injured during placement.

There's one risk we want you to understand clearly: the nerve in the lower jaw that gives feeling to your lower lip and chin. If you're missing a lot of bone, placing an implant without crowding that nerve gets harder. Irritation can cause tingling, numbness or a complete lack of sensation in your lip, chin or tongue. It usually resolves with time, but it can be permanent, so tell us about any numbness as soon as possible.

How long will the implants last?

Usually a very long time. For patients missing all of their teeth, long-term studies spanning more than 30 years show an 80 to 90 percent success rate. For patients missing one or several teeth, recent studies put success above 95 percent, which compares favorably with implant replacements elsewhere in the body, like hips and knees.

And if one of your dental implants doesn't heal properly or loosens over time? It can be removed, and once the site heals, another implant usually takes its place.

When your replacement teeth are attached

Your new teeth go on once your jaw bone has fused firmly to the implant, and in some cases that phase starts immediately or shortly after placement. The restorative work begins with specialized impressions that let Dr. Burns produce a replica of your mouth and implants, plus bite records that show how your upper and lower jaws relate.

From there we make the abutments, the support posts that connect your replacement teeth to your implants. Often an "off the shelf" abutment works. Other times we craft custom abutments from gold or tooth-colored ceramic, which adds cost and time but earns its keep in fit. If your final restoration is a removable denture, we'll also build the custom support bars, snaps, magnets, or clips that hold your teeth to the implants.

How do I clean my new teeth?

Just like natural teeth: brush, floss, and use any other aids we recommend, every day. Plan on visiting several times each year for hygiene and maintenance too. Like any tooth replacement, implants and their components see wear and tear, so expect the occasional tune-up over the years: clip replacement, relines, screw tightening, and small adjustments. That's normal, and it's how your investment keeps working for decades.

How much does dental implant treatment cost?

Honestly? Dental implants come with significant benefits, but also significant cost. When you weigh your options, keep two things in mind: longevity, since implants last a lifetime while other restorations need replacement every decade, and your quality of life. You may be surprised to find that in the long run, dental implants are often the more affordable choice.

Because no two cases match, we can't quote the restorative phase to the dollar on day one, but you'll receive a reasonable estimate from our office before work begins. Read our Cost of Dental Implants page for the factors that shape the number, or call 540-740-8937 to find out if you're a candidate.

Frequently Asked Questions

What patients ask after implant placement

How much does dental implant treatment cost?

Implants come with significant benefits, but also significant cost. Weigh the longevity, since implants last a lifetime while other restorations need replacing every decade, and in the long run implants are often the more affordable choice. You'll get a reasonable estimate from our office, and our Cost of Dental Implants page explains the factors. Call 540-740-8937 to see if you're a candidate.

What are the potential problems after dental implant surgery?

Most patients don't experience severe or significant pain, and you'll have pain medication and antibiotics to ease recovery. Occasionally an infection needs extra antibiotics, an adjacent tooth is injured, or the lower-jaw nerve is irritated, causing tingling or numbness in the lip, chin, or tongue. That usually resolves with time, but report any numbness to us right away.

When are the replacement teeth attached to the implant?

Once your jaw bone has fused firmly to the implant, though some cases start immediately after placement. We take specialized impressions and bite records, make your abutments, and craft your teeth in the lab. A few teeth can take as few as three short appointments; a removable denture, up to five. Overall, treatment finishes 1 to 12 months after placement.

What can I use for teeth while the implants heal?

Many options, tailored to you: temporary removable teeth, a temporary bridge, a modified or new temporary denture if all your teeth are missing, or temporary transitional implants with teeth inserted the same day. Some implants can be "loaded" immediately, so a replacement tooth goes on the day of placement or shortly after.

How long will the implants last?

A long time. Studies over 30 years show 80 to 90 percent success for patients missing all their teeth, and greater than 95 percent for one or several teeth, comparable to hip or knee implants. If an implant fails to heal or loosens, it can usually be removed and replaced after the site heals.

How do I clean my new teeth?

Like natural teeth: brush, floss, and use any recommended aids daily, plus several hygiene visits each year. Expect routine wear-and-tear upkeep over the years, such as clip replacement, relines, screw tightening, and adjustments.

Who will be performing my procedure?

You'll have a team dedicated to your care: Dr. Burns, our office and clinical staff, and for some stages trusted colleagues who perform or advise, all working toward the best possible result.
