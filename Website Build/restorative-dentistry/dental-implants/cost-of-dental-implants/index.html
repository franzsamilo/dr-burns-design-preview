The math dentures and bridges don't show you

When you weigh the cost of dental implants against other tooth replacement methods, factor in longevity. Dentures and dental bridges are less expensive at first, but that affordability can be short-lived. These older methods need repair and replacement every 5-10 years, which often makes them more expensive over time. A dental implant, properly placed and cared for, can last a lifetime. You pay for it once.

The cost no quote mentions: your jaw bone

When a tooth is missing, the jaw bone under it deteriorates. A denture or bridge may seem to work like a tooth, but beneath the surface the bone keeps eroding, and eventually the structure of the face changes with it. That distortion can mean additional cosmetic costs down the road.

Dental implants are made of titanium, which integrates with the jaw bone, strengthening it and stimulating bone growth. The implant preserves the natural strength and quality of your mouth, which heads off problems before they get expensive.

An investment in how you live, not just what you pay

A dental implant is the closest thing to a natural tooth. That makes it a long-term investment in quality of life, not only money. You keep eating the same healthy foods you've always enjoyed. And you're done with the day-to-day hassles, and the quiet embarrassment, that dentures so often cause. No quote can put a number on that part. Patients tell us it's the part that mattered most.

Comparing quotes? Make sure both count these six steps

Placing a dental implant involves several steps, and often more than one professional. A lower quote sometimes just means steps got left off the page. Before you compare two numbers, confirm each one includes:

  • Exams and office visits
  • Tooth or root extraction
  • Bone grafting, if your case needs it
  • Placement of the titanium root, the implant itself
  • Placement of the crown
  • X-rays and pre and post operative care

Two places to look before you write a check

First, check with your dental insurance carrier to see what portion of dental implants they cover, if any. We'll help you find out before treatment starts. Second, health care credit companies offer no-interest and low-interest loans for medical procedures, dental implants included, so the cost can become a monthly payment instead of one lump sum.

The cost of dental implants, question by question

How much do dental implants cost at your office?

There's no honest one-size answer, because no two cases involve the same steps. The only way to receive an accurate price quote is to come in for a consultation, where we can give you a specific price for your case. Call 540-740-8937 to schedule. The consultation is free, and the number you leave with covers every step.

Aren't dentures or a bridge cheaper than an implant?

At first, yes. Over time, usually not. Dentures and bridges typically need repair and replacement every 5-10 years, and those repeat bills add up. A dental implant that's properly placed and cared for can last a lifetime, so the higher first cost is often the lower lifetime cost.

What should a complete implant quote include?

Six things: exams and office visits, tooth or root extraction, bone grafting if needed, placement of the titanium root (the implant itself), placement of the crown, and X-rays with pre and post operative care. If a quote you're comparing skips any of these, it isn't the full price.

Does dental insurance cover implants?

Sometimes, in part. The first step is to check with your dental insurance carrier to see what portion, if any, they cover. We do that with you before treatment starts, so whatever benefits you have get used.

What if I can't pay for implants all at once?

Health care credit companies offer no-interest and low-interest loans for medical procedures, including dental implants. That turns the cost into a monthly payment, and we'll walk through the options with you at your consultation.
