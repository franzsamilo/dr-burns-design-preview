Dental Implants: Changing Lives

Anchoring artificial teeth in the jaw makes them as strong as natural teeth. You eat what you want, and you never worry about your teeth slipping when you talk, eat, or laugh. Here's how that works, question by question.

What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is an artificial tooth made up of three parts. The implant itself is a small titanium screw placed in your jaw. An abutment, or connector, joins the implant to the crown, the third and only visible part. So when you hear "implant," don't picture a whole fake tooth being screwed in. Picture a root, a connector, and a tooth built to match your smile.

How do dental implants work?

Titanium has a rare talent: it bonds permanently with living bone through a process called osseointegration. Once the implant fuses with your jawbone, it holds like a natural tooth root. That fusion is so effective that implants handle everything your old teeth did, chewing, biting, corn on the cob, all of it, with no dietary asterisks.

What are the advantages of getting dental implants at Jeffrey S. Burns DDS?

Most offices send your crown to an outside lab and send you home to wait. Not here. An in-house CEREC machine lets the team scan the implant on the day of your surgery, fabricate the implant crown right in the office, and place it once the implant is ready. In other words, every part of the treatment happens in only 2 appointments: surgery and placement. Dr. Burns pairs that technology with meticulous attention to detail, starting with a 3-D scan of the implant site, designing the perfect location and size for the implant, then printing a surgical guide on the office's 3-D printer.

Will I need to have bone grafting?

Here's the honest rule of thumb: patients who've had a missing tooth for a month or more usually need bone grafting before implant surgery, because the jawbone starts shrinking as soon as the root is gone. A graft adds about 4 months to the usual 4-6 month timeline. Every mouth is different, though. A consultation reveals the particulars of your case, and treatment gets tailored accordingly, never copied from the last patient's chart.

Do dental implants hurt?

No. Surgery is performed under your choice of a variety of anesthesia options, and the surgical site is always kept numb during the procedure. Later in the day, as the anesthesia wears off, you may notice some mild discomfort, nothing over-the-counter medication can't handle. Plan to take the rest of the day off after your procedure. Most patients are back at work the next day, which surprises nearly everyone who asks.

Frequently Asked Questions

The short version: every question at a glance

Do dental implants hurt?

No. Surgery is performed under your choice of a variety of anesthesia options, and the surgical site is always kept numb during the procedure. As the anesthesia wears off later in the day, any mild discomfort is easily controlled with over-the-counter medications.

How long does it take to get a dental implant?

The entire process usually takes 4-6 months, with an additional 4 months if your case requires bone grafting. Thanks to the in-house CEREC machine, the treatment itself takes only 2 appointments: surgery and placement.

Will I miss work?

Plan to take the rest of the day off following your implant procedure. Most patients are able to return to work the next day with very little discomfort.

Will I need to have bone grafting?

On average, patients who've had a missing tooth for a month or more require bone grafting before implant surgery. A consultation reveals the particulars of your case. Every patient is unique, so treatment is tailored accordingly.

What is a dental implant?

A dental implant is an artificial tooth made up of three parts: a small titanium screw placed in your jaw, an abutment (the connector), and the crown, the visible part of the implant. Together they look, feel, and work like a natural tooth.

How do dental implants work?

Implants are made of titanium, which permanently bonds with the jawbone through a process called osseointegration. That fusion is so effective that implants function like natural teeth in all chewing, biting, and dietary capacities.

What are the advantages of getting dental implants at Jeffrey S. Burns DDS?

An in-house CEREC machine scans the implant on the day of your surgery and the crown is fabricated right in the office, so everything happens in 2 appointments. Dr. Burns plans each case from a 3-D scan and prints a surgical guide on the office's 3-D printer before surgery day.
