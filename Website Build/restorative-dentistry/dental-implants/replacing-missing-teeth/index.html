A missing tooth lets the jaw literally melt away

Here's the part nobody warns you about. Once a tooth is gone, the bone that held it has no work left to do, so the body starts reclaiming it. Most people lose 25% of their supporting jawbone structure within the first year after tooth loss. The jaw literally melts away. Meanwhile your remaining teeth carry extra load, wear out prematurely, and can crack or drift. Some patients pick up headaches and jaw pain on top of it. Who would want their appearance and health to deteriorate like that? Replacing the tooth early keeps the whole cascade from starting.

The fixed bridge: sturdy, but healthy teeth pay for it

A fixed bridge is a connected set of replacement teeth cemented on top of the teeth beside the gap. It stays put and it works. The trade-off lands on the neighbors: their protective outer layer is ground down so the bridge can seat, which means two healthy teeth give up structure for a space they didn't create. That's a permanent change, and it's worth understanding before you agree to it.

The flipper and the metal partial: stopgaps that come out at night

The quickest, cheapest fix is a flipper: a removable plastic tooth on a plastic retainer. It's inexpensive, it's fast, and it's fragile. Think of it as a placeholder, not a plan. One step sturdier is the metal partial, a removable partial denture cast in metal and plastic and held in place by wire clips. You can take it out and put it back in whenever you need to. What neither one does is give the bone underneath a job, so the jaw keeps shrinking while you wear them.

Complete dentures: common, not always comfortable

For people missing all of their teeth in one or both jaws, complete dentures are the most common answer. Some people adapt to them well. Others find them uncomfortable, even intolerable, because jaws come in different sizes and shapes and a denture can only grip so much. Removing a partial every night gets old, and a denture that slips mid-sentence is more than inconvenient. It's embarrassing. If that's where you've landed, you don't have to stay there.

Dental implants: the permanent option that guards the bone

Dental implants are the most comfortable and permanent solution. A small post takes over for the missing root, forming a strong foundation that keeps the jaw healthy and strong. Implants can support a single replacement tooth or secure a full denture firmly in place. Unlike a bridge, no healthy teeth are damaged. And unlike most bridges, an implant can last a lifetime. They're also easiest to place when a tooth is first extracted, because bone replacement grows more complex the longer the site sits empty.

Who's a candidate? Age is not a factor.

A good candidate is anyone missing one or more teeth, or anyone unhappy with their dentures. Age is not a factor. What does matter: smoking, diseases such as diabetes, and radiation therapy to the area have been shown to lower implant success rates, so the conversation starts with your health history instead of a sales pitch. Jaw X-rays show whether your bone will accommodate implants, and detailed imaging fills in whatever else is needed to place them properly. We focus on smile-enhancing restorations, and if another option serves you better, Dr. Burns will say so plainly.

Choosing a tooth replacement, answered honestly

Quick answers to what patients ask in the chair. For deeper implant questions, our dental implants FAQs page goes further.

How much does replacing a missing tooth cost?

It depends on which option fits your mouth, so we don't quote blind. Your free consultation ends with an honest number for your case, in writing. For a full breakdown of what goes into implant pricing, see our cost of dental implants page.

What happens to my jaw if I don't replace a missing tooth?

The jaw literally melts away. Most people lose 25% of their supporting jawbone structure within the first year after tooth loss. Your remaining teeth also take on extra work, so they wear out prematurely or get damaged, and some people develop headaches or jaw pain. Replacing the tooth early stops that process before it starts.

What is a flipper, and is it a real solution?

A flipper is a removable plastic tooth on a plastic retainer. It's inexpensive and quick, and it's also fragile and strictly temporary. It fills the gap cosmetically while you plan something permanent, but it does nothing to stop the bone underneath from shrinking.

How is a metal partial different from a flipper?

A metal partial is a removable partial denture cast in metal and plastic, held in place by wire clips. It's less fragile than a flipper and you can remove and reinsert it whenever you need to. It's still a removable appliance, though, so it won't preserve the jawbone the way an implant does.

Why would I choose an implant over a fixed bridge?

A bridge is cemented onto the teeth next to the gap, and those healthy teeth must be ground down to anchor it. An implant stands on its own, so no healthy teeth are damaged, and unlike most bridges it can last a lifetime. It also keeps the jawbone working, which a bridge can't do.

Am I too old for dental implants?

No. A good candidate is anyone missing one or more teeth, or anyone unhappy with their dentures, and age is not a factor. What can lower the success rate is smoking, diseases such as diabetes, and radiation therapy to the area. Jaw X-rays tell us whether your bone will accommodate implants before anything is decided.
