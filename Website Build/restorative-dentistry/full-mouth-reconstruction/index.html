A necessary series of procedures, not an elective surgery

Full mouth reconstruction refers to the process of completely restoring all of the teeth in your mouth. That means the whole mouth: upper teeth, lower teeth, and the bite that connects them.

Unlike a “smile makeover,” which is elective, reconstruction is a necessary series of procedures. It's what a mouth needs when it has stopped working, and when fixing one emergency at a time only spreads the damage around.

The four signs a mouth needs rebuilding

Reconstruction is needed when teeth have been lost, when teeth have been injured or fractured, when teeth have become worn as a result of acid-erosion, or when jaw pain is present due to bite problems.

One of those is a repair. Two or more usually means the problem is the whole system, not a single tooth.

Procedures involved in full mouth reconstruction

Every patient's needs are different and, therefore, require different procedures. Usually, full mouth reconstruction requires multiple office visits and some amount of healing time. Dr. Burns will determine which procedure, or combination of procedures, is necessary to restore and rebuild your perfect smile. Some options include:

Teeth cleaning (prophylaxis)

A professional teeth cleaning is often where the rebuild starts. Healthy, clean gums and teeth give every procedure that follows a better foundation.

Crown lengthening

Prepares tooth structure for possible crowns or bridges by exposing more of the tooth above the gumline.

Orthognathic surgery

Surgery to reposition the jaw, for cases where the bite problem lives in the jaw itself rather than in the teeth.

Contouring of the gum tissue

Reshapes the gumline so the rebuilt teeth sit naturally and evenly when you smile.

Reduction of natural tooth structure

Prepares the teeth to receive crowns, bridges, and veneers.

Temporary restorations

Placed to get you used to your new teeth or bite adjustment before anything permanent is cemented.

Permanent restorations

The finished work: crowns, veneers, inlays/onlays, or bridges, matched to what each tooth needs.

Braces

Move teeth into the ideal position for reconstruction, so restorations aren't built on a crooked foundation.

Bone or soft tissue grafting

Increases the stability of your teeth or implants where the supporting bone or gum has thinned.

Temporary teeth first, permanent teeth second

The sequence is deliberate. Dr. Burns places temporary restorations to get you used to your new teeth, or to a bite adjustment, while your mouth heals around them.

Once the fit has proven itself in daily life, the permanent restorations go in: crowns, veneers, inlays/onlays, or bridges. You never gamble your bite on work you haven't lived with.

Grafting and braces: the work that makes it last

Some rebuilds need reinforcement before restoration. Bone or soft tissue grafting increases the stability of your teeth or implants. Braces move teeth into the ideal position for reconstruction.

Neither step is glamorous. Both are the difference between work that lasts and work that gets redone.
