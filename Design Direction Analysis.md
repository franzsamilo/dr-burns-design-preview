# Design Direction Analysis

Created from the Jeffrey Burns knowledge base, current website archive, UVP docs, Bobbie/Dr. Burns feedback, media library, and extracted wireframes.

## Executive diagnosis

The problem is not that the client lacks direction. The direction is buried in negative feedback. They are rejecting designs that feel dark, generic, over-spaced, low-contrast, clinical, and visually detached from the existing Jeffrey Burns brand.

The design needs to become: **light, legible, grey/white/teal, outcome-focused, emotionally reassuring, proof-dense, and direct-response oriented.**

This is a premium dental implant site for older, anxious patients considering a major life-changing procedure. The site should not feel like a luxury black-label brand, a hospital brochure, or a generic dental template. It should feel like a trusted local expert with a proprietary smile system, real patient proof, and a clear path to call or schedule.

## What the client is really asking for

### 1. Stay inside the existing brand color world

They are not asking for a new visual identity. They repeatedly point back to the current site palette:

- White
- Light grey
- Jeffrey Burns grey: `#3e3e3e`
- Teal: `#74bdc2`

Rules:
- Remove heavy black from the main design language.
- Use `#3e3e3e` as the deepest neutral.
- If using grey variation, go lighter, not darker.
- Teal should be an accent and attention color, especially for CTAs and icons.
- Avoid grey text on grey backgrounds unless contrast is very clear.

### 2. Design for older readers

The target patient is likely older and may have weaker eyesight. The feedback repeatedly flags small type, low contrast, and dark backgrounds.

Rules:
- Body copy should be large and high contrast.
- Important labels/titles should be noticeably larger.
- Avoid white text on black/dark sections for long copy.
- Buttons, phone number, and CTA areas must be obvious.
- The “Your Journey” title and step labels need larger type.

### 3. Reduce scrolling and section bloat

The client complaints about large gaps and oversized sections are conversion complaints, not just layout preferences. Leads are waiting too long to see proof, benefits, and next steps.

Rules:
- Compress vertical spacing.
- Convert long paragraphs into bullets.
- Avoid sections that take an entire screen unless they are the hero.
- Every 1 to 2 scrolls should reveal either proof, a benefit, or a CTA.
- The comparison, Burns Protocol, and patient journey sections need to feel concise, not ceremonial.

### 4. Use outcome imagery, not clinical imagery

The client is very clear that procedure imagery causes anxiety. They want patients to picture the outcome, not the surgery.

Use:
- Full-face smiling patients
- Warm patient/dentist interactions
- Dr. Burns smiling in doctor attire
- Before/after photos where useful
- Diagrams or clean illustrations for procedure education
- DreamSmile™ watermark/logo on patient smile images where appropriate

Avoid:
- Drills
- Close-up surgery
- Bloody or shocking dental visuals
- Scary filling/procedure photos
- Dark grey overlays that make photos dull
- Generic stock patients when actual assets exist

### 5. Make DreamSmile™ the hero brand

The client is not asking for “dental implants” as a generic service page. The site should sell DreamSmile™ as the named outcome powered by The Burns Protocol.

Rules:
- Use `DreamSmile™` consistently.
- Use the official DreamSmile™ logo where the feedback calls for it.
- Headline: `A DreamSmile™ You'll Never Have to Hide`
- The comparison table should make DreamSmile™ visually dominant.
- “The DreamSmile” text should often become the DreamSmile™ logo.

### 6. Lead with substantiated proof, not vague premium language

The UVP docs reject generic claims like “we care” and “state of the art.” The site must use specific proof.

High-value proof to surface:
- 30+ years implant experience
- Dr. Burns teaches other dentists nationwide
- Proprietary Burns Protocol
- 98%+ success rate
- Personal cell phone for DreamSmile™ patients
- Same-day temporary teeth
- One-stop shop
- On-staff anesthesiologist
- DreamSmile™ Lifetime Warranty / tiered warranties
- DreamSmile™ Care Kit
- TV/radio “As Seen On” trust bar
- Video testimonials and before/after proof

### 7. Every page should behave like a landing page

Except resources/blog-style pages, each important page should be direct-response oriented.

Rules:
- Phone number visible at top.
- Free consultation / call CTA visible early.
- One primary conversion path: call or schedule.
- Use phone number under “Schedule Your Free Consultation.”
- Don’t bury the CTA under lots of education.
- Don’t use huge external-source buttons. Paraphrase references as native content and hyperlink inline or list references at bottom.

## What to stop doing

- Stop using heavy black sections as the premium move.
- Stop putting grey boxes on grey backgrounds with weak contrast.
- Stop relying on red glow icons that look dull instead of intentional.
- Stop making sections oversized because the layout feels “luxury.”
- Stop using clinical/scary dental imagery.
- Stop forcing patients through long scrolls before they see proof or next action.
- Stop treating each service page as informational SEO content only.
- Stop using generic mockup/guide imagery when actual TIE/DreamSmile media exists.
- Stop repeating CTAs inside every patient-journey step.
- Stop linking patients out through large medical-reference buttons.

## What to preserve

- The existing grey/white/teal brand DNA.
- The warm local-practice credibility from the current site.
- The dental operatory / real-practice authenticity, but brighten and modernize it.
- The direct phone/contact emphasis.
- Patient video testimonials from the media library.
- Real Dr. Burns/patient assets.
- The current site’s service depth, but reorganized into clearer landing-page templates.

## Recommended visual direction

### Direction: Bright clinical trust, not dark luxury

Use a clean white and light-grey base with teal highlights and dark grey text. The site should feel premium because it is clear, spacious enough, proof-rich, and human, not because it is black or dramatic.

Suggested palette roles:
- Background: white / near-white
- Section alt: very light cool grey
- Text: `#3e3e3e`
- Accent/CTA: `#74bdc2`, with darker teal for hover/contrast where needed
- Borders/dividers: pale grey
- Avoid: pure black as large section background

### Layout language

- Split sections: solid light background + clean image on the other side.
- White cards on light grey backgrounds when cards are necessary.
- Use comparison tables only when the comparison is immediately obvious.
- Use bullets for benefits and process sections.
- Use larger step labels and icons with strong contrast.
- Use a concise patient journey, not a scroll-heavy timeline.

### Image language

- Full-face smiling patient outcomes.
- Dr. Burns smiling, in doctor attire.
- Warm patient/dentist interactions.
- Before/after and diagrams for education.
- Minimal overlays. If text must sit near an image, use split layout instead.

## Homepage section-level direction

1. **Top bar / header**
   - Phone number in upper right.
   - No “Home” nav item.
   - Nav: Dental Implants, General Dentistry, Cosmetic Dentistry, Meet the Doctors, Resources, Contact.
   - CTA: Schedule Free Consultation or Call Dr. Burns.

2. **Hero**
   - Headline: `A DreamSmile™ You'll Never Have to Hide`
   - Subheadline: tie DreamSmile™ to a healthier, long-lasting, natural-looking smile designed by Dr. Burns.
   - CTA: Schedule Your Free Consultation.
   - Phone number directly under CTA.
   - Use a warm real patient/Dr. Burns image or bright practice/patient outcome visual, not a dark operatory overlay.

3. **Immediate proof bar**
   - 30+ years experience
   - 98%+ success rate
   - Teaches doctors nationwide
   - DreamSmile™ warranty
   - Same-day temporary teeth

4. **Problem / patient fears**
   - Denture slippage
   - Facial aging
   - Cost confusion
   - Dental anxiety
   - Fear of corporate/cookie-cutter treatment
   - Keep this tight and empathetic.

5. **DreamSmile™ introduction**
   - Use official DreamSmile™ logo.
   - Explain the outcome: healthy, beautiful, natural-looking, permanent smile.
   - Mention The Burns Protocol as the mechanism.

6. **Comparison table**
   - DreamSmile™ vs Traditional Dental Implants.
   - DreamSmile™ logo should visually lead the table.
   - Avoid tiny text.
   - Include hidden fees/no warranty language exactly per feedback.

7. **The Burns Protocol / patient journey**
   - Compress into clear steps.
   - Use larger titles.
   - Only one CTA after the final Dream Smile Assessment / journey step.
   - Use bullet copy and one warm patient image.

8. **Dr. Burns authority section**
   - Use smiling doctor-attire photo.
   - Nationally recognized, teaches doctors, 30+ years, personal cell phone.
   - Human warmth plus authority.

9. **Social proof section**
   - Video testimonials from downloaded media.
   - Before/after where available.
   - “As Seen On” TV/radio trust bar replacing filler content.

10. **Lead magnet / free guide**
   - Use the actual TIE/DreamSmile guide asset.
   - Do not use generic guide mockup or unrelated patient.

11. **Final CTA**
   - Schedule consultation.
   - Phone number repeated.
   - Reassure: no pressure, clear cost, see if you qualify.

## What needs to happen next

### Priority 1: Create a one-page design rules brief

Before anyone designs another mockup, lock these rules:
- Palette: white/light grey/teal/`#3e3e3e`, no heavy black.
- Minimum readable type sizes for older audience.
- No scary clinical images.
- Split layouts over grey photo overlays.
- DreamSmile™ naming and logo usage.
- Proof density and CTA placement.

### Priority 2: Build a reusable page template system

Because “every page should be a landing page,” make templates, not one-off pages:

- Homepage template
- Dental implant landing page template
- Full mouth / DreamSmile™ page template
- General service page template
- Cosmetic service page template
- Resource/blog page template
- Contact/consultation page template

Each page template needs:
- Hero with benefit + CTA + phone
- Proof element above or near fold
- Fear/objection section
- Benefit section
- Proof/testimonial block
- FAQ or education block
- Final CTA

### Priority 3: Do an asset pass before redesigning

The redesign should be asset-led. Pull and mark:
- Best Dr. Burns smiling photo
- Best full-face patient smile images
- Best video testimonials
- TV/radio logos for As Seen On
- Official DreamSmile™ logo
- Actual guide mockup / TIE-created assets
- Before/after images
- Procedure diagrams or clean illustrations

### Priority 4: Rebuild the homepage direction in two constrained variants

Do not show open-ended styles. Show two variants inside the same rules:

- **Variant A: White-first**
  - White sections, teal CTA, light grey cards.
  - Best for clarity and older audience readability.

- **Variant B: Light-grey editorial**
  - Light grey section rhythm, white cards, teal accents.
  - Slightly more premium/structured, still no black.

Both variants should avoid heavy black and clinical imagery.

### Priority 5: Create a feedback checklist so comments stop feeling vague

Every future mockup review should be checked against:

- Does it use DreamSmile™ correctly?
- Does it avoid black-heavy sections?
- Is the text readable for older patients?
- Is every section compact enough?
- Is proof visible early and often?
- Are images outcome-focused?
- Is the CTA/phone easy to find?
- Does it feel like Jeffrey Burns, not a generic dental chain?

## Bottom line

The direction is clear: **make the site feel like a bright, premium, trustworthy DreamSmile™ conversion site for older implant patients.** The design should use the existing Jeffrey Burns palette, compress the scroll, increase readability, replace clinical imagery with human outcomes, and make the proprietary proof impossible to miss.
